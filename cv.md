## Research profile

Marine biologist with more than 14 years of experience at the interface of ecophysiology and genomics, studying how climate change affects marine organisms, with an emphasis on commercially and ecologically important shellfish and aquaculture species. My research integrates physiological assays (metabolic rate, biomechanics, stress tolerance) with genomic and epigenomic approaches (RNA-seq, bisulfite sequencing, SNP analysis) to investigate the mechanisms underlying responses to ocean acidification, marine heatwaves, and hypoxia. As a research lead, I have managed multidisciplinary teams of up to 16 staff, administered program budgets totaling $1.8M, and secured competitive funding from NSF, USDA, and NOAA. My work has resulted in 20 peer-reviewed publications, including articles in *Nature Climate Change* and *Global Change Biology*, and has been featured in *The Atlantic*, *Scientific American*, and *Popular Science*.

## Employment {#employment}

<div class="cv-job" markdown="1">
### Fish and Wildlife Biologist IV, 2023-present
<div class="cv-org">Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife, Fish Program, Intergovernmental Ocean Policy Division &middot; Montesano, WA</div>

- Direct research on climate change impacts to commercial shellfish fisheries valued at more than $120M annually; designed monitoring programs for eight recreational fisheries.
- Principal investigator and unit lead of the Coastal Shellfish Unit: 16 staff, eight program budgets totaling $1.8M, and research grants from solicitation through procurement and delivery.
- Produce quantitative assessments of harvest, effort, and biological data and deliver evidence-based management recommendations to agency leadership, policymakers, tribal co-managers, and the Fish and Wildlife Commission.
- Lead interagency and stakeholder coordination, convening advisory groups and partnering with federal, state, and tribal entities on fishery management and harvest plans aligned with long-term sustainability and climate resilience.
</div>

<div class="cv-job" markdown="1">
### Research Scientist, 2020-2023
<div class="cv-org">Cooperative Institute for Climate, Ocean, & Ecosystem Studies (joint appointment): University of Washington School of Aquatic and Fishery Sciences and NOAA Northwest Fisheries Science Center &middot; Seattle, WA &middot; Supervisors: Steven Roberts, Mackenzie Gavery</div>

- Led multidisciplinary fisheries and aquaculture research with state and federal agencies, universities, tribal organizations, and industry, translating results into operational recommendations.
- Used genomic (DNA, RNA-seq), physiological (metabolic rate), biochemical (ATPase activity), and behavioral (filtration rate) assays to identify traits associated with growth and environmental resilience in Pacific oysters.
- Investigated how triploidy affects DNA methylation (bisulfite sequencing), growth, survival, and stress tolerance in Pacific oysters.
- Identified gene markers associated with attachment, growth, and survival to support broodstock selection in two mytilid mussel species, and SNPs associated with phenotype and social behavior in sockeye salmon.
- Assessed nutritional state and reproductive status of oysters, Manila clams, and geoduck with histology.
- Analyzed large genomic datasets from Pacific oysters, geoduck, and Manila clams in Bash, R, and Python on the UW high-performance computing cluster.
- Secured funding from PSMFC, USDA (NRSP-8 and SRGARP), and NOAA (NOPP); authored peer-reviewed papers and technical reports.
- Facilitated recurring discussions on pathways to address race- and place-based inequity and to integrate the knowledge and goals of Indigenous partners in research.
</div>
