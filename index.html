Fish and Wildlife Biologist IV · Coastal Shellfish Unit Lead
Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife

I study how climate change affects marine organisms, with an emphasis on commercially and ecologically important shellfish. My work combines physiological assays (metabolic rate, biomechanics, stress tolerance) with genomic and epigenomic approaches (RNA-seq, bisulfite sequencing, SNP analysis) and quantitative fisheries assessment to understand responses to ocean acidification, marine heatwaves, and hypoxia, and to turn that understanding into management advice.

What I work on

Fisheries science Coastal shellfish fisheries in a changing ocean Climate-driven recruitment of Pacific razor clams, Dungeness crab meat-fill forecasting and larval monitoring, and harvest estimation for Washington's coastal recreational fisheries. Aquaculture Climate resilience in Pacific oysters How triploidy shapes stress responses, DNA methylation, and mortality during marine heatwaves, and how hatchery conditioning can improve seed survival. Ecomechanics Mussel attachment under ocean change How acidification, warming, and hypoxia weaken the byssal threads mussels use to hold on, from adhesive chemistry to gene expression and raft aquaculture. Open science Open code, data, and lab notebook Analysis pipelines in R, Stan, and Bash are public on GitHub, and day-to-day lab work is documented in an open online notebook.
